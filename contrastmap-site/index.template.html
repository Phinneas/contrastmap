The science of the swing

Cold plunge vs infrared sauna

Two opposite stressors, two different payoffs. Here's how they compare — and why doing both is the whole point of contrast therapy.

Cold plunge

39–55°F
Does what
Constricts blood vessels, calms inflammation, and floods you with alertness-boosting norepinephrine.
Best for
Post-workout recovery, mood & energy, sharpening focus, building mental grit.
Session
2–5 minutes, often 1–3 rounds.
Feels like
Sharp, bracing, then a clean rush of euphoria.
VS

Infrared sauna

110–150°F
Does what
Raises core temperature, dilates vessels, and drives a deep sweat to boost circulation.
Best for
Relaxation, muscle tension, circulation, a detoxifying sweat, and better sleep.
Session
20–45 minutes at a lower, gentler heat than a traditional sauna.
Feels like
Deep, radiant warmth that loosens everything up.

Why do both? The vascular pump.

Alternating heat and cold makes your blood vessels expand and contract like a pump — the idea behind contrast therapy. A typical ritual: 10–15 min in the sauna, then 1–3 min in the plunge, repeated 3 rounds, always finishing on cold. Studios below are built for exactly this.
