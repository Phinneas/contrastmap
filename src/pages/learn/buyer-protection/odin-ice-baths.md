**Odin Ice Baths was a real Australian company that built cedar ice baths. It went into voluntary administration on April 1, 2025, according to a notice filed with Australia's corporate regulator, ASIC. Customers have publicly reported paying in full or in part for baths that were never delivered. If you are looking at an Odin product today, the first thing to confirm is who is selling it and who would honor the warranty.**

I'm not issuing a verdict here. Below is the public record, checked against our [Pre-Purchase Scam Checklist](/learn/buyer-protection/scam-checklist), so you can decide for yourself.

## Why the payment structure matters

Odin sold on a deposit model with long lead times. That means buyers paid before their bath was built. When a company enters administration with open orders, deposit holders become unsecured creditors. They usually recover little or nothing. This is the core risk with any cold plunge sold on a long pre-order, from any brand.

## What happened after administration

- Several Australian competitors, including KOUD and Helsi, publicly offered discounts or credits of up to AUD 3,500 to Odin customers. Those are marketing offers, not refunds.
- An Australian recovery-center owner described plumbing new third-party chillers into existing Odin tubs after the collapse. Existing tubs can still work. Odin warranty support is the open question.
- I could not confirm from public records whether a buyer took over the Odin brand. If someone is selling Odin products now, ask who owns the business and whether they honor pre-administration warranties.

## A note on "Nordhaus"

Our original research brief paired Odin with a brand called Nordhaus. I found no public record linking the two. If you've seen the name used alongside Odin products, treat it as unverified until a seller can show you the business registration.

## Before you buy any pre-order plunge

1. Avoid large deposits on long lead times when you can.
2. If you must pay a deposit, use a credit card, and note the date your dispute rights expire.
3. Search the company's name on the national business register (ASIC in Australia, your Secretary of State in the U.S.) before you pay.
